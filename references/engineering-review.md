# Engineering review by circuit type

Read only applicable sections. Record each consequential finding as requirement → evidence/input → calculation or observation → margin → action. Prioritize functional or destructive faults before optimization. An unresolved value stays unresolved; use a labeled range for exploratory design.

## Power, switching supplies and motors
- Build a rail budget across operating modes: input range/transients, startup/inrush, peak and average load, duty cycle, regeneration, sequencing and fault states. Check regulator stability requirements, UVLO, current limit and output discharge against the exact datasheet.
- Size the complete delivery and return chain: connector/contact, fuse, protection device, copper necks, vias, shunts and thermal relief spokes. Use actual copper thickness, length, layer and temperature-rise assumptions with a documented current-capacity method. Parallel vias do not guarantee equal current sharing.
- Calculate voltage drop and resistive loss with R = ρL/A, ΔV = IR and P = I_rms²R, including temperature-dependent resistance. A wide pour does not remove a bottleneck at a pad or connector.
- For MOSFETs, use R_DS(on) at actual gate voltage and expected temperature, SOA for the pulse duration, switching conditions, dead time, diode recovery and gate-driver capability. V_GS(th) is not a fully-on specification. P_cond ≈ I_rms²R_DS(on) is only conduction loss; switching, gate and diode losses need separate estimates/models.
- For current sensing, verify shunt tolerance/tempco/pulse rating, amplifier common-mode range and settling, Kelvin connections, gain and ADC range. Calculate hardware/firmware trip limits including tolerances. Explicitly flag disabled regulation, blanking delays and unverified fault response.
- Check motor stall/startup, shoot-through, inductive kick, braking/regeneration, supply absorption and reverse polarity. Derive clamp/snubber/protection requirements from energy and waveforms; do not prescribe an arbitrary TVS or RC value.
- Check capacitor effective capacitance at bias/temperature, dielectric class (`C0G/NP0` for timing/RF/precision; derate `X7R/X5R` for DC bias and temperature; avoid `Y5V/Z5U`), ESR, ripple rating, self-resonance (capacitors turn inductive above SRF; inductors turn capacitive above SRF), and lifetime; inductor saturation (ferrites drop sharply past `I_sat`, powdered iron rolls off softly) and RMS heating; diode voltage/current/recovery. Part selection: resolve every claim to a real part number and read the datasheet table (min/typ/max, test conditions); label values guaranteed vs typical vs read-from-plot; a bin table lists the family, not your reel. Compare in the same quantity, recompute LED/driver current over Vf min..max, and when asked "what value" give one value with its 2–3 assumptions. Polarity can disagree between datasheet, library symbol and 3D mark: report it and confirm in the assembler's 3D preview. Trace what the board does when a control input floats. After a value change update schematic, PCB fields, netlist, generators, BOM/CPL, then ERC/DRC with parity. Do checks you can do yourself; hand over only what needs the order page or real measurements.

Read bulk-capacitor ripple rating from the manufacturer's frequency multiplier table at the actual switching/PWM frequency; a headline value quoted at 100 kHz can be several times too high for low-kHz PWM (e.g. multiplier 0.3 at 1–10 kHz). Verify tolerances at worst input/load rather than nominal operation alone, and check divider sensitivity before rounding calculated feedback/timing values to standard E-series resistors.
- Estimate thermal losses and heat flow through package, copper, vias, enclosure and airflow. `θ_JA` is test-board dependent and often assumes ideal test-board cooling; `T_J ≈ T_A + Pθ_JA` is only a preliminary estimate. When heatsinking, build the thermal circuit model (`R_th,tot = R_th,JC + R_th,TIM + R_th,sink`, `ΔT = P_diss · R_th,tot`) and size thermal via arrays explicitly (a single small via has high thermal resistance, e.g. ~180 °C/W for 10 mil). Account for skin effect (`δ = √(ρ / (π μ f))`) on high-frequency AC/switching conductors. Specify where and how to measure hot spots.

## Signal integrity and power integrity
- Classify nets using transmitter edge rate, topology, route delay and interface requirements, not clock frequency alone. Obtain the actual stackup, dielectric/copper geometry and manufacturer impedance tolerances before claiming controlled impedance.
- Derive differential impedance, skew/length, topology, termination and via/stub constraints from the exact interface/device guide. Include package/connector delay when required. Do not apply universal 50/90/100 Ω or length limits to unrelated interfaces. For off-board or long runs where single-ended SPI/I2C degrade from wire capacitance and ground bounce, evaluate differential buses (e.g. CAN/RS-485). For RF/high-speed microstrips, avoid sharp 90° bends, cross unavoidable adjacent-layer traces perpendicularly, and isolate coupled traces with ground pours/via fences.
- For an at-risk channel, use appropriate IBIS/SPICE/field-solver models if available. Record model/version, excitation, corners, loads and acceptance mask/timing limits. A missing model means the simulation is unavailable; do not present a hand estimate as a completed simulation.
- Review supply tolerance, DC drop, transient droop, regulator response and decoupling loop inductance. Z_target ≈ allowed rail excursion / load-step current is an initial PDN target, not proof of bandwidth performance. Account for capacitor ESR/ESL and anti-resonance when relevant; blindly adding capacitors or ferrites can worsen response.

## Precision analog, RF and EMC
- For analog paths, budget offset, bias/leakage, gain/reference errors, noise bandwidth, source impedance, ADC acquisition/settling and tolerances. Check common-mode/input/output swing across temperature and supply corners. Choose layout/filter changes against the error budget.
- For EMC, identify source → coupling path → victim, then assess loop area, cable/common-mode currents, connector filtering, shield/chassis termination and ESD return routes. Reference app notes guide design; they do not certify compliance.
- For RF/antennas, use the module/antenna manufacturer's stackup, ground, feed and keepout guidance. Matching values depend on the final board/enclosure; require tuning/measurement when needed. Do not claim RF performance from a generic reference footprint.

## Isolation, reliability and serviceability
- For hazardous voltage or protective isolation, identify applicable current standards and working/transient voltage, insulation class, pollution degree, material group, altitude and environment. Derive creepage/clearance and barrier details from those inputs. Use board cutouts (slots) to extend surface creepage where spacing is tight, and round HV pads/corners to reduce electric-field concentration, corona and surface carbonization/tracking. Do not guess spacing from voltage alone or claim certification from DRC.
- Review credible single faults, power-off/backfeed, unplugged or reversed connectors, shorts, open sensors, stalled loads and watchdog/reset states. Rank failure severity and choose protection/test points proportional to the application.
- Record unresolved safety, sourcing and lifetime risks, acceptable substitutes with verified pin/package/function compatibility, and measurable verification needed before release.

## Primary references
Use the device's own datasheet/layout guidance first; these explain mechanisms rather than universal design numbers.
- [TI SLVA959B — motor-driver layout](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slva959b/slva959b.pdf): power/gate/sense paths and thermal layout.
- [TI SCAA082A — high-speed layout](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/scaa082a/scaa082a.pdf): edge rates, return paths and coupling.
- [Analog Devices AN-136](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/app-notes/an-136.html): switching-power current loops, sensitive nodes and copper.
